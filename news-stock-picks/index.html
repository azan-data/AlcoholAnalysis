How the score works

Every input is shown in each stock's detail row
News (0–100)
Each headline is tagged with the sectors and stocks it moves, from −3 to +3. A tag on the stock counts in full, a tag on its sector counts 60%. Each headline's weight halves every 7 days. The sum s becomes 50 + 50·tanh(s/3).
Trend (0–100)
Where the price sits in its 52-week range: 0 at the low, 100 at the high. Stocks near their 52-week highs have tended to keep outperforming (George & Hwang, 2004).
Discount (0–100)
How far the price is below its 52-week high, with 35% or more scoring 100. This is the contrarian side, the opposite bet to Trend: recent losers tend to bounce in the short run (Jegadeesh, 1990). Profiles decide how much each style counts.
Earnings (0–100, when reported)
The latest quarter against analyst estimates: a big beat with raised guidance scores 100, a miss 25. Stocks that beat tend to keep drifting up for weeks (Bernard & Thomas, 1989). Left blank for companies that haven't reported Q3 yet.
Macro fit (0–100)
A judgment call by Claude on how the sector fits today's backdrop: oil above $100, a 5% 10-year yield, 3.7% inflation, an active U.S.–Iran conflict and midterms on Nov 3. The reasoning appears in each stock's detail row.
Risk penalty
Volatility is estimated from the 52-week range, σ ≈ ln(high/low) ÷ 1.6 per year, and an earnings report within 10 days adds 15 points. The penalty is risk × aversion × 0.3.

Score = weighted average of the available factors − risk penalty. Missing factors drop out and the remaining weights are rescaled. Buy ≥ , Accumulate ≥ , Hold ≥ , Avoid below.

Typical 3-month move is one standard deviation, σ ÷ 2, estimated from the 52-week range. About two times in three, a stock has moved less than this over three months.
